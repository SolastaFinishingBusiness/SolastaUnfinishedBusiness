- Dungeon Maker, for campaign authors: a campaign can now use campaign variables with reserved names as commands. Dialog_Trigger starts an NPC's or object's dialogue, Roll_Trigger has a character roll a Roll Activator's check, Teleport_Node and Teleport_PartyNode teleport one character or the party to a named gadget (usually a Node), placed nearby as the game's own Teleporter does it, Torches_Lit, Torches_Auto and Torch_<name> switch torches, and the Clock_ variables read the time, count minutes, hours and days, and skip time. A roll or a one-character teleport acts for the character whose move set off the Area Activator or pressure plate, or who used the object, also when that gadget activates another gadget (such as a Variable Operation) that writes the variable, through any number of gadgets, and on through a variable or quest step a Variable or Quest Activator reacts to, a dialogue that gadget starts and the actions of its lines, and a Timed Activator it enables (the targets of a Roll Activator act for the character who rolled, and a button set off by damage for the party member who hit it); the mod's own writes, such as the _Status variables, start no chain; when the party arrived together, or the start can't be traced, it acts only if exactly one eligible character is involved. Without one such character it does nothing. When a command fails, a message at the top of the screen says so (for a roll or a one-character teleport, what started it, such as a timer, which can't say who), and the mod's log has a detailed line on what wrote the variable, through which gadgets, and why. Optional <command>_Status variables report Pending, Running, Succeeded, Failed or Cancelled. Players need the mod for a campaign that uses them. They work in every campaign while the mod is installed; a campaign that doesn't create these variables is not affected. The guide is Documentation/DungeonMakerCommands.md on the mod's GitHub page. Thanks to BlackguardRogue for the Dungeon Maker tools this comes from.
- Dungeon Maker: the campaign editor, the variable picker, the Cheats > Campaign Variables panel and the location editor's gadget, prop and room palettes each have a new bar at the top of their lists to search and sort them. Typing in the search field shows only the entries whose name or title contains the text, and Clear empties it; the sort dropdown orders the list by the campaign's own order, by name, or by title (by type for the variables panel), and the arrow beside it switches between ascending and descending. Each list also has a sort of its own kind, which comes first, with the bar's sort breaking ties: in the campaign editor a "Sort:" dropdown and arrow directly under each list's header, and on the variable picker and the variables panel a second row under the bar's sort. It starts on Default (the bar's sort alone) and offers keys such as hit points, challenge rating or an ability score for monsters and NPCs, cost for items, start level or map size for locations, or a variable's starting value. While a list is sorted by a field its rows don't show, each row shows its value at its right end; sorted by campaign start, ascending, the starting location comes first. The sort and its direction are shared by the three screens and remembered, and saved as soon as they are chosen; the search is emptied each time a screen opens. If the search hides the selected entry, the selection is dropped. In the campaign editor, folding a list away, by its header or by opening another list with a shortcut icon, drops that list's selection too. Only what is shown changes: the campaign keeps its own order, so nothing is saved differently and auto-start quests still start in the same order. The editor's move arrows only work in the plain view (own order, ascending, no search). In the palettes the sort orders the blueprints inside each category, the categories keep the game's order, and the search hides categories with nothing left to show. A "Categories:" toggle on the sort row (in the palettes at its right end, showing only Default or A-Z, with "Categories" in its tooltip) switches the order of the sections themselves (the editor's lists, the palettes' categories) between the game's order and alphabetical by title. A palette keeps each category folded or open as it was when the location's environment or size changes; a category the new environment adds starts folded. Mod UI > Dungeon Maker has a setting for each of these screens, and under it one for each tool it has (Search, Page sort, Category sort, Category-specific sort), all on by default; switching a screen off switches its tools off, and a screen with its tools off looks and works as without the mod. The setting "Enable assets sorting on Dungeon Maker editor" is gone: the palettes' Default is the game's own order, and the campaign editor no longer re-sorts the campaign's own lists when it opens.
- Dungeon Maker custom weapons: the weapon tooltip's damage bonus now shows the weapon's total flat bonus, the enhancement of the item it was made from plus the number typed in its Weapon Effects damage box. A +1 weapon with -1 typed in showed +1, and a plain weapon with +2 typed in showed no bonus. The damage breakdown of an attack no longer lists the two when they cancel each other, also when the game gives the enhancement no name (as on the Main Attack box); holding Alt shows the full breakdown, and Gameplay > Campaigns > "Hide weapon damage bonuses that cancel each other out" (on by default) can turn this off. It applies to any weapon whose own damage bonus cancels the enhancement of its item, though in practice only Dungeon Maker custom weapons have such a pair.
- Breakdowns (damage, attack, armour class and others): an entry the game listed with no source, shown as "+1:" with nothing after it, now names its source: the item, spell or character it came from, the weapon of the attack shown, or else the kind of source ("+1: Item").
- Weapon tooltips: the damage bonus row showed the weapon's attack bonus. It now shows its damage bonus (they only differ on weapons whose two bonuses are not the same).
- Not tested in game: the Dungeon Maker campaign commands (no campaign here uses them yet), commands through gadgets activated by other gadgets, through variables, quest steps, dialogues, timers and damaged buttons, the failure messages, the custom weapon tooltip and damage breakdown with Alt, entries named after their source, the per-screen tool settings, and anything on two computers. The list search and sort were tested in single player.
